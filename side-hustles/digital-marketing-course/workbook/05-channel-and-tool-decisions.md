# Channel and tool-selection guide

[Course home](../README.md)

This guide compares functions and tradeoffs, rather than prescribing a paid stack or freezing current vendor features. Check current official capabilities, fees, policies, and export terms before choosing a service.

## Match the channel to the decision

| Channel | Useful context for developer products | Main creative work | Useful signal | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn | Professional identity, technical explanations, team relevance | Specific posts, demos, conversations | Relevant replies and qualified visits | Reach and audience fit vary |
| YouTube-style long-form video | Demonstrations needing sequence and depth | Clear teaching and readable recording | Appropriate viewing and resource visits | Production and maintenance time |
| Short-video feeds | One visible problem or useful demonstration | Strong framing, understandable payoff | Relevant continuation and visits | Broad reach may have weak purchase fit |
| Search articles | People actively asking a question | Complete, current answers | Relevant query visits and next steps | Discoverability and timing are uncertain |
| Email | Continuing a voluntary relationship | Useful teaching and accurate offers | Replies, purchases, customer outcomes | Permission, delivery, and list relevance |
| Technical communities | Specific problems and peer discussion | Direct helpful participation | Useful conversations and permitted visits | Local rules and promotion limits |
| Partnerships | A related trusted audience | Shared useful material | Qualified responses and contribution | Fit and coordination matter more than size |
| Marketplaces | Buyers browsing within an existing catalog | Clear listing, previews, product quality | Net contribution and buyer fit | Fees, competition, limited relationship control |
| Paid search/social | Bounded acquisition experiments | Relevant creative and destination | Confirmed contribution after acquisition | Cash risk and measurement limits |
| Team relationships | Shared organizational learning needs | Scoped proposal and appropriate delivery | Qualified pilots and successful use | Coordination and purchasing complexity |

These are channel archetypes. They do not claim current superiority of a named platform. Begin where your actual buyers are reachable and where you can repeatedly create useful material. Add a channel to resolve a known constraint.

## Choose a minimal operating stack

| Function | Minimum capability | Questions to verify before choosing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public pages | Clear sample and product information | Can I edit titles, navigation, mobile layout, and commercial terms? |
| Checkout | Appropriate payment and confirmation | Which countries, currencies, methods, fees, refunds, and buyer protections apply? |
| Digital delivery | Reliable authorized access | Can buyers recover access, and what happens if a link fails? |
| Email | Subscription, delivery, opt-out, export | Does authentication, suppression, segmentation, and appropriate consent work? |
| Measurement | Defined visits and confirmed orders | Can I deduplicate, reconcile, minimize data, and respect applicable choices? |
| Recording | Clear screen and intelligible audio | Can I frame code, capture reliable takes, and correct captions? |
| Support | A monitored contact path | What response and data-handling commitments can I meet? |
| Backups/versioning | Recoverable source material and buyer commitments | Can I export my work and keep edition history? |

An integrated platform reduces connection work but may constrain exports, formatting, or commercial terms. A collection of separate tools offers flexibility but creates integration and support work. Compare the actual tradeoff for one product before building a complex system.

## Marketplace versus your own storefront

A marketplace can provide catalog discovery and built-in transaction features. It may also limit pricing, customer communication, design, and access to customer relationships. Your own storefront gives more control while making you responsible for discovery and more operational decisions.

Neither is universally better. Ask where your buyers already look, which commercial functions you can operate, and what margin remains. Do not treat marketplace availability as guaranteed traffic. Do not treat a custom domain as proof that you have distribution.

If a service claims to act as merchant of record, inspect what that means in its current agreement and supported regions. Do not assume that one phrase resolves every tax, refund, privacy, or business obligation you have.

## Build versus buy

As a developer, you may enjoy building a custom checkout or course platform. That work can be worthwhile if it solves a real constraint. It can also postpone research and create avoidable maintenance.

Before building, name the capability an existing approach lacks, how much that limitation costs, and what operating burden the custom solution adds. A simple product page and established transaction path may be enough to validate an offer. Learn where buyers struggle before investing in infrastructure they will not notice.
