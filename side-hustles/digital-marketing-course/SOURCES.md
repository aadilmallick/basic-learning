# Sources and freshness notes

[Course home](README.md)

Primary sources checked on **November 13, 2026**. These pages support specific current platform and U.S. compliance statements. The course's broader examples, planning frameworks, pricing assumptions, and schedules are original instructional synthesis. They are not empirical findings attributed to the sources below.

## R1: Google Search

**Title:** SEO Starter Guide. **Publisher:** Google Search Central.

[Official documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

Used in Lesson 7 for the distinction between useful, understandable public content and guaranteed search inclusion or ranking. The page is a general SEO foundation, not evidence that a particular article will attract traffic. Search presentation, indexing, and ranking systems can change. Check the current documentation and your own site's diagnostics when implementing.

## R2: LinkedIn video

**Title:** Share videos on LinkedIn. **Publisher:** LinkedIn Help.

[Official documentation](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7494039)

Used in Lesson 6 for caption options, interface safe zones, and the inability to directly choose immersive-feed placement. No claim is made that vertical video, a particular duration, or a posting time guarantees distribution. The course's 45–90 second exercise and canvas sizes are editorial choices. Verify current upload limits in this source before export.

## R3: Email delivery

**Title:** Email sender guidelines. **Publisher:** Gmail Help.

[Official documentation](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)

Used in Lesson 8 for authentication and the distinction between general and bulk-sender requirements. The source concerns delivery to personal Gmail accounts; other providers can have their own rules. It does not guarantee inbox placement or supply consent to send marketing. Configure authentication using your sending provider's current exact instructions.

## R4: Reviews and testimonials

**Title:** The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers. **Publisher:** Federal Trade Commission.

[Official guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

Used in Lessons 4 and 12 for avoiding fabricated reviews, false testimonials, and incentives conditioned on positive sentiment. This is introductory U.S. guidance, not an individualized compliance assessment. Actual testimonial use depends on context and other applicable rules.

## R5: Commercial email

**Title:** CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business. **Publisher:** Federal Trade Commission.

[Official guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

Used in Lesson 8 for a concise summary of commercial-email requirements, including B2B applicability, truthful message information, a postal address, and opt-out handling. The course's explicit-opt-in operating approach does not imply that the same legal standard applies everywhere. Check the guide and applicable jurisdiction before sending.

## R6: Campaign links

**Title:** URL builders: Collect campaign data with custom URLs. **Publisher:** Google Analytics Help.

[Official documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952)

Used in Lesson 9 for UTM meanings and consistent, case-sensitive campaign labels. The course uses a fictional example URL. Adding parameters does not by itself install analytics or guarantee attribution. Verify implementation in the tool you actually use.

## R7: Attribution

**Title:** Select attribution settings. **Publisher:** Google Analytics Help.

[Official documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10597962)

Used in Lesson 9 for credit-assignment settings and key-event attribution. Interface labels and reporting capabilities can change. The course teaches concepts without relying on a fixed sequence of interface clicks. Attribution reports are not direct measurements of incremental causal impact.

## R8: Endorsement disclosures

**Title:** Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers. **Publisher:** Federal Trade Commission.

[Official guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)

Used in Lesson 10 for material connections and understandable, noticeable disclosures. An example disclosure is a drafting aid, not a determination that every placement is compliant. Evaluate the actual medium, audience, and relationship.

## What was not verified

No creator's private analytics, revenue, audience composition, or sales system was accessed. No platform vendor was evaluated through a live paid account. No advertisement, email campaign, storefront, product purchase, or public launch was executed. Product-specific taxes, merchant-of-record arrangements, privacy configurations, and international consumer rules were not determined. Prices and performance assumptions in lessons are explicitly fictional.

Refresh platform details before implementation. Read the relevant official terms before choosing paid software; this course intentionally does not freeze vendor prices or recommend a paid stack that has not been evaluated for your actual needs.
