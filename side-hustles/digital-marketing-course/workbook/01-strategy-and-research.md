## 3. Interview guide

**Opening:** I am learning how people approach this topic. I want to understand a recent experience, rather than ask you to predict whether you would buy an idea. May I take notes? [If you want to record, ask separately and use an appropriate process.]

1. Tell me about the last time this topic caused a real problem.
2. What were you trying to do?
3. What made the problem difficult?
4. What did you try, and in what order?
5. What helped, and what remained unclear?
6. What time, money, or coordination did the attempt involve?
7. Have you paid for a similar learning resource? What influenced that decision?
8. Is there an important question I did not ask?

**Notes after the conversation:** observed trigger; desired progress; current alternatives; constraints; actual spending behavior if volunteered; uncertainties; contradictory evidence.

## 6. Positioning statement

## 8. Product readiness review

Can a new reader follow the prerequisites into the first lesson? Is the first useful result clear? Do examples and explanations agree? Do you have rights to the material? Can someone navigate it after the first read? Are version assumptions visible? Does the sample accurately represent the paid experience?

Mark each question **verified**, **needs revision**, or **not yet inspected**. A sample review does not establish demand; it establishes that you have a coherent experience worth testing.
