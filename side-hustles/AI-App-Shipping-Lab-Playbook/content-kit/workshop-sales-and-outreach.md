# Workshop sales page and outreach


# 15 The free workshop run of show and sales script

Title: From AI Prototype to a Testable App.

Duration: approximately 55 minutes. The teaching is useful without a purchase. Use the reference app, the ten-case sheet, a prepared recovery path, and one audience exercise.

## Minutes zero through three Set the outcome

Say:

> By the end of this session, you should be able to take one uncertain behavior in an AI app and turn it into a concrete check.
>
> We will use a small brief-drafting application with fictional notes. We are not testing anyone’s private client data.
>
> This is aimed at people who already know basic JavaScript. You can follow the reasoning even if you do not run the code today.
>
> At the end, I will explain an optional paid project lab. First we will do the exercise.

Ask attendees to write one sentence about the app or feature they are trying to build. Give them a quiet minute rather than filling every pause with talk.

## Minutes three through eight Recognize the problem

Show the normal demo, then ask what should happen when the note lacks a deadline.

Say:

> The application can produce a convincing draft. The question is whether it preserves what the user did and did not say.
>
> Before running the incomplete example, write the behavior you expect. Should the deadline be invented, left unknown, or asked about?
>
> We need that decision before we judge the result.

Read a few volunteered responses. Do not manufacture agreement or pretend that a quiet chat is active.

## Minutes eight through fifteen Trace the workflow

Draw interface, server, model, response check, editable draft, review, and export.

Say:

> These boxes help us put each responsibility somewhere concrete.
>
> The model generates a candidate response. Validation checks aspects of the data. The interface represents uncertainty and review. The human still decides whether the draft reflects the intended project.
>
> If the output is wrong, the fix may belong in a prompt, a validation rule, the interface, or the scope of the workflow. We should inspect the failure before choosing.

Have attendees identify one boundary in their own app they cannot currently explain.

## Minutes fifteen through twenty-seven Run the cases

Use C01, C02, and C03. State each expectation first. Record the actual result.

Say:

> A normal example tells us something useful. An incomplete example asks a different question. A contradiction asks another.
>
> I want the app to surface the conflict rather than silently choose for the client.
>
> Here is what this version actually did. We will keep that observation even if it makes the demo less tidy.

If an external service fails, use the prepared recording or mock and label the switch. Teach the same concept without claiming the live call succeeded.

## Minutes twenty-seven through thirty-five Improve one thing

Show an actual small change or a clearly identified teaching branch. Compare the same cases.

Say:

> We have one observed issue and one intended correction. I want the change small enough to review.
>
> Here are the files that changed. Here is the behavior check. Now we run the same cases.
>
> This does not prove general reliability. It gives us evidence about the particular behavior we changed.

## Minutes thirty-five through forty Let people apply it

Prompt:

> Write one input, the behavior you expect, the evidence you would inspect, and the smallest next change if the current app fails.
>
> You have two minutes. Keep it small enough to test today.

Invite two or three volunteers, depending on attendance. Give useful feedback on the check, not an improvised full code audit.

## Minutes forty through forty-seven Explain the paid offer

Say:

> You can use today’s checklist on your own. The public walkthrough and notes will help you repeat the process.
>
> If you want a longer project path with assignments and feedback, I’m opening AI App Shipping Lab.
>
> The first sprint is six weeks. We will build Client Brief Copilot from a specification through implementation, evaluation, failure handling, deployment, and handoff. The aim is to understand and demonstrate the application, not just finish a video.
>
> You should already know basic JavaScript and Git and have built a small web app. Expect three to five hours of work each week.
>
> Founding membership is $149 per month and continues until cancelled. Because the sprint lasts six weeks, it normally spans two monthly charges, or $298 if you stay through both.
>
> The membership includes one weekly group session, structured questions, and one scoped 10-minute individual review per billing month. It does not include unlimited private consulting or emergency support.
>
> I am limiting the founding group to 15 because I want to keep the review workload manageable. The program page shows the actual remaining availability, start date, session schedule, refund policy, and cancellation route.
>
> The lab does not promise employment, client contracts, or income. The result we are working toward is a project you can explain, test, and hand off within the defined training scope.
>
> Read the full details before joining: [program link]. If it does not fit, keep using the free materials.

Show the actual program page and terms. Do not create a countdown during the session unless it reflects a real deadline.

## Minutes forty-seven through fifty-five Questions

For “Can I start without knowing JavaScript?” say:

> This cohort assumes basic JavaScript and Git. I would rather help you choose a preparatory path than put you into a group that moves too quickly.

For “Will this help me make money?” say:

> The program teaches a defined project and engineering process. I cannot promise that it will produce clients, a job, or income. Decide based on whether the skills and support are worth the price for your situation.

For “Why pay when the videos are free?” say:

> The videos teach the concepts. The membership adds the sequence, assignments, working group, and the specific feedback described on the page.

For “Can you review my whole application?” say:

> The individual review is scoped to a small change or identified issue and the stated time budget. A full application audit is outside this membership.

Close by restating the learner’s next useful action, whether or not they buy.

# 17 Get the first ten members without an audience

Publishing begins the discovery process, but your first cohort should not depend on a recommendation feed producing strangers on command.

## Build a small relevant contact list

Identify 30 people or introduction paths. Use your existing relationships, developer groups where you participate, public discussions of the specific problem, and event organizers. Your school and professional background can suggest communities to approach; it does not imply those communities endorse you.

For each, record the observed reason for fit, the appropriate contact route, and whether follow-up is welcome. Do not harvest private member lists or treat extension users as marketing subscribers.

## Send a transparent interview invitation

> I’m developing a small project-based workshop for JavaScript developers who have used AI coding tools but get stuck reviewing or finishing the result.
>
> Have you tried that kind of project recently? I’m looking for a 20-minute conversation about the actual obstacles before I finalize the pilot.
>
> If it fits, I may share the paid program details afterward. There is no obligation to join.

Ask what they attempted, where they stopped, what they tried, what they paid for, what would count as success, and how much time they can invest. Ask permission before sending the pilot.

## Make the paid offer clearly

> Based on what you described, the Client Brief Copilot sprint may fit. You would work through a small AI app with a specification, evaluation cases, failure handling, and handoff.
>
> The founding subscription is $149 per month and continues until cancelled. The six-week sprint normally spans two charges. The page explains the schedule and bounded support.
>
> Would you like the full details so you can decide?

Do not imply that an interview answer commits someone to purchase. Send a checkout link only with accurate terms and a ready product.

## Follow up once with a useful question

> You asked about [specific issue]. The program page now explains that part of the project and the review scope. Here is the link you requested: [program link].
>
> If the timing or format does not fit, no problem. I would appreciate knowing which part made it unsuitable.

If they decline, stop selling. Record the reason without arguing.

## Use a genuine introduction

> I’m looking for developers who already know JavaScript and have recently tried an AI-assisted app project. I’m running a small teaching pilot about reviewing and finishing the result.
>
> If someone comes to mind, would you be comfortable forwarding the workshop details? Please do not share anyone’s contact information without their agreement.

## Approach a host with a complete proposal

> I have a 55-minute workshop called From AI Prototype to a Testable App. Participants inspect a small application, practice writing expected behavior, and leave with a checklist.
>
> Your group appears relevant because [specific audience observation]. I can provide the outline, prerequisite note, starter examples, and a recording arrangement if appropriate.
>
> The teaching session ends with a brief, optional invitation to my paid project group. Would you like to review the outline?

Seek five suitable host contacts per week during a focused outreach period. One relevant hosted session can be a better experiment than posting on five feeds.

## The decision after ten qualified offers

If five or more appropriate people pay, deliver the small pilot and observe. If two to four pay, inspect objections and test one change. If zero or one pays, pause course production and revisit the buyer, outcome, credibility, format, or price.

These are local decision rules for a selected warm sample. They do not predict public conversion rates.

If you do not reach the published minimum cohort size, follow the fallback and refund terms you communicated. Do not quietly replace a paid group program with something materially different.
