# Copy-and-edit freelancing kit

[Guide home](README.md)

These are drafts and business planning aids, not legal contracts. Replace every bracketed field, verify every factual claim, and adapt to the actual recipient and jurisdiction before use. No message has been sent. Example prices and schedules are hypotheses. Use the outreach requirements in chapter 2, including a valid business postal address and opt-out for US commercial email. Never include confidential client information in a public example.

## 1. One-page offer record

**Buyer:** [one specific role at one type of organization].

**Problem:** [repeated task and observable friction, with evidence or explicitly labeled hypothesis].

**Result:** [one change the buyer can observe].

**Deliverables:** [supported workflow, environment, format, and handover].

**Acceptance:** [representative examples and important failure behavior].

**Excluded:** [additional sites, roles, features, data handling, or ongoing responsibilities].

**Proof:** [one relevant demo and your exact contribution].

**Price experiment:** [USD amount or range] based on [scope and total effort].

**Payment:** [milestones and due dates to agree].

**Acquisition:** [specific channel and next action].

**Review:** [date and evidence that would justify continuing, changing, or stopping].

## 2. Extension service page draft

### Browser tools for teams doing repetitive work

I build focused Chrome extensions and small web integrations that make one workflow easier to complete. We agree on the supported pages, data handling, and acceptance examples before implementation.

My existing work includes tools for video notes, exports, and browser recording. [Insert your own relevant demo and precise role.]

A small engagement can include a workflow review, a scoped pilot, a tested demonstration, source handover, and operating notes. Larger integrations, multiple supported sites, AI features, and ongoing maintenance are quoted separately when needed.

Tell me which task your team repeats, what system it happens in, and who uses it: [contact route].

**Publication check:** Confirm your current availability, claims, links, and actual offer before publishing. Do not present a proposed service as a proven customer outcome.

## 3. Case study skeleton

### [Task or workflow improved]

**Context:** [What the user needed and how the task worked before.]

**My role:** [What you personally designed, implemented, or explained.]

**Scope:** [Supported environment and deliverables.]

**Demonstration:** [Approved screenshots or synthetic demo.]

**Important decisions:** [Why this approach, permission scope, error handling, and alternatives considered.]

**Evidence:** [Tests, accepted behavior, or measured result with method/date.]

**Limits:** [What was not measured, supported, or included.]

**Relevant next step:** [Invitation to discuss a similar, bounded task.]

Get written permission before naming a client, sharing material, or quoting feedback. For personal projects, label them personal projects. For resume claims, confirm current evidence before turning them into public marketing metrics.

## 4. Warm introduction draft

Hi [name], I am taking on a small number of scoped browser extension and web integration projects. Here is a short example: [proof link].

I am looking to speak with [role] at [business type] who has [specific workflow problem]. If someone comes to mind, would you be comfortable introducing us? This description is easy to forward: [two-sentence service description].

**Use only with an actual relationship.** Do not imply a prior collaboration that did not happen. Adapt commercial identification and required footer to the actual message and channel.

## 7. Technical tutorial pitch

**Subject:** Tutorial proposal: [reader outcome]

Hi [editor/content owner],

I build [relevant technical work] and write practical guides. I am exploring an example for [reader] that produces [working result] using [documented product capabilities].

I reviewed [specific content] and would first confirm whether this overlaps material you already have planned. The proposed deliverable is [article scope], [repository scope], and [optional demo], with [review limit] and a tested setup.

[Identity, valid postal address, commercial identification, and opt-out as applicable.]

Attach an outline only if useful. Do not claim a missing feature or documentation gap without research. Agree to attribution, usage rights, and revisions before writing the commissioned piece.

## 8. Follow-up and close-the-loop drafts

**First follow-up:** “One clarification on my note: the initial work could be limited to [useful small scope], rather than a full system replacement. [Relevant proof or question.] Is this in your team's area, or should I close the loop?”

**Final follow-up:** “I will close the loop here. If [specific need] becomes relevant later, [proof link] shows the kind of scoped work I do. No need to respond if it is not a priority.”

Keep required identification and opt-out information on commercial emails. Stop after refusal, opt-out, or the planned limited sequence. A follow-up is not permission to switch channels repeatedly.

## 9. Twenty-minute fit-call agenda

| Time | Focus | Useful prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 minutes | Purpose | Let's understand the task and decide whether a small next step makes sense. |
| 2–8 minutes | Recent example | Walk me through the last time someone did this. |
| 8–12 minutes | Importance and alternatives | How often does it happen, what goes wrong, and what have you already tried? |
| 12–16 minutes | Constraints | Who approves it, what is the timing, and what budget scale is realistic? |
| 16–20 minutes | Decision | Is this a fit, what remains unknown, and who does what next? |

Do not treat the timing as rigid. The deliverable is a decision and next action, not completion of every question at any cost.

## 15. Handover index

- Accepted result, version, date, and supported environment.
- Customer account ownership, billing, and access roles.
- Clean setup, build, configuration, and deployment instructions.
- Public configuration versus secrets; no secret values in the document.
- Acceptance evidence and representative test data.
- Known limitations, failure recovery, and rollback process.
- Recurring services, usage costs, and monitoring responsibilities.
- Correction period, support route, response expectation, and optional maintenance.
- License/ownership notes and approved reusable foundations.
- Confirmation of removed temporary access and handled test data.

## 16. Referral and case-study request

“Now that [accepted result] is in use, I would value your feedback on what improved and what still needs attention. If you are comfortable, may I prepare a short case study for your approval using [proposed public details]? I will not publish private information or unapproved metrics.”

Separately: “If you know another [buyer type] dealing with [specific task], I would appreciate an introduction. Here is a short description you can forward: [offer].”

## 17. Weekly business review

Record actual accounts researched, approaches, replies, qualified conversations, proposals, commitments, collected cash, expenses, and hours. Distinguish warm, partner, outbound, content, and platform sources.

For each active opportunity, record its next action and owner. For each completed project, compare estimated and actual total hours and explain the largest variance. Decide one change to test next week. Do not interpret hypothetical funnel math or an unsigned proposal as income.

## 18. AI assistant prompts you can adapt

### Prospect research draft

“Using only these supplied public pages, summarize the organization's actual services, cite each observation with its URL, and identify a possible fit for my browser workflow offer. Separate facts from hypotheses. Do not infer budget, staffing shortages, purchasing intent, or a relationship. Produce a short account brief and three verification questions. Do not contact anyone.”

### Proposal review

“Review this draft against the attached discovery notes. Find promises without evidence, ambiguous acceptance criteria, unbounded support, missing dependencies, and inconsistent pricing arithmetic. Suggest precise edits. Do not invent legal protections or claim enforceability. Return a concise issue table and a revised business scope.”

### Extension implementation brief

“Implement only [agreed behavior] in this approved repository using synthetic fixtures. Inspect existing conventions first. Preserve permission boundaries, do not add remote executable code, and do not embed private credentials. Explain the plan, make a reviewable change, run the relevant checks, and report actual commands and remaining limitations. Do not deploy or submit the extension.”

### Tutorial verification

“Follow this tutorial from a clean documented environment using the approved test accounts. List prerequisites and versions, run each command where authorized, compare expected and actual output, and report failures. Distinguish code inspected from code executed. Do not publish or expose credentials.”

These prompts are starting instructions, not tool actions performed by the guide. Review the inputs and the assistant's output yourself.
