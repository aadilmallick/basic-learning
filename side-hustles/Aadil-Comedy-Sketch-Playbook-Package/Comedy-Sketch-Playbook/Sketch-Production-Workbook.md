## 5. Performance and edit review

- The situation can be understood without an introduction from me.
- Each character wants something.
- The unusual rule is coherent enough for later consequences to connect.
- The middle changes the pressure rather than repeating the opening.
- The listener has tactics and reactions, not only repeated disbelief.
- The eye directions suggest one conversation.
- The important prop or expression is visible on a phone.
- Captions do not reveal the punchline prematurely.
- Useful pauses remain; accidental waiting is removed.
- The ending registers, and the scene stops decisively.
- Dialogue is clear and music/asset permissions fit each use.
- The exported versions have been watched in the actual platform preview.

## 8. Twelve extra premises to develop

These are starting ideas, not audience-tested scripts. Develop the rule and ending before filming.

| Premise | Possible internal rule | Direction for a button |
|---|---|---|
| The friend who charges a storage fee for returning your charger | Every favor is an invoice | You receive a bill for reading the bill |
| The person who admits being wrong only during a promotion | Accountability must protect their image | The apology expires before the sentence ends |
| A five-minute favor has a full onboarding process | Helping requires institutional permission | The favor is over before training finishes |
| The roommate who calls a mess a personality test | Responsibility can be reframed as judgment | Their own test results demand cleaning |
| The friend who needs perfect gear to start a simple hobby | Preparation is a substitute for participation | They buy a recovery product before doing the activity |
| The person who takes jet lag seriously after moving from the sofa to bed | Every minor transition is a major journey | They ask the roommate to meet them at arrivals |
| The group chat has an appeals court for ignored messages | Every social interaction is evidence | The judge is the person who never replies |
| An overly literal lost-and-found clerk | Proof of possession is required before returning lost things | The only proof is inside the missing bag |
| The friend who wants praise for almost helping | Intention deserves a reward | They ask for a thank-you note for considering reading yours |
| A delivery app rates your ability to receive food | The customer is an underperforming contractor | Your own door declines your application |
| The person who rehearses looking surprised at a gift | Every emotion requires direction | The giver asks for another take before handing it over |
| The neighbor asks for feedback on the complaint process | Every problem must become a satisfaction metric | The form only offers compliments |

## 9. Ten production tricks worth testing

1. Open on a line that already implies the relationship and problem.
2. Give the grounded character a visible task.
3. Keep the unusual person's logic sincere.
4. Let a new consequence do the heightening.
5. Record the listening performance.
6. Use one simple visual identifier per role.
7. Capture a readable insert of the final prop.
8. Test several timing gaps instead of removing all silence.
9. Protect the reveal from premature captions.
10. End on the actual payoff, with only the reaction it needs.
