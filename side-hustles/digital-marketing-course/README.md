# Digital Marketing for Developer Products

## Sell useful coding notes, guides, courses, and workshops

A practical, first-principles course for a solo developer who wants to turn technical knowledge into a sustainable product business. Start with customer problems and an accurate offer, then build content, video, search, email, sales, and a reliable customer experience.

This is a complete teaching course, rather than just a curriculum outline. It contains 12 substantial lessons, worked numerical examples, a recurring product case, reusable worksheets, an operating checklist, a glossary, and a capstone. The accompanying HTML edition contains the same Markdown teaching material in a single offline reader.

**Prepared:** November 13, 2026. **Audience:** A developer beginning digital marketing. **Prerequisites:** A subject you can teach accurately, willingness to talk to prospective buyers, and basic familiarity with a web page and email. No previous marketing background or paid software is assumed.

The fictional **Coroutine Field Guide** is the running example because it makes the decisions concrete for coding notes. Substitute your own subject. All prices, metrics, sales, and customer conversations in illustrative examples are invented teaching cases, not benchmarks, actual research findings, or earnings forecasts.

## What you will be able to do

- Find a specific buyer and investigate a recurring problem.
- Turn personal notes into a clearly scoped learning product.
- Compare formats, calculate contribution, and choose a defensible price hypothesis.
- Write a sales page with evidence, prerequisites, and understandable terms.
- Create useful LinkedIn posts, demos, and short technical videos.
- Build search articles and a voluntary email relationship.
- Measure a customer journey, interpret attribution, and avoid weak experiments.
- Evaluate partnerships, affiliates, paid channels, and team offers.
- Rehearse delivery, launch a bounded offer, and improve customer outcomes.

## How to use the course

Read in order the first time because later chapters use earlier decisions. Keep one product as your running case. You can take one lesson per week and apply it, or study several related lessons together before building. The twelve-week schedule in Lesson 12 is an optional application rhythm, not a promise that every product can be created in that time.

The application activities are optional and have no mandatory quizzes. They become especially useful when you want a real launch rather than familiarity with terminology. You can keep everything private until your product, terms, and operations are ready.

Open the HTML edition in a browser for a single-file reading experience, navigation, search, and printing. Open the Markdown files in any Markdown editor, or place the folder in a repository. No package installation, account connection, external assets, or server is needed to read the course. Live links require internet access. Templates are editable Markdown files in the workbook folder.

## Scope and evidence

The course focuses on marketing your own educational products. It introduces broad digital-marketing disciplines through that use case; it is not a certification, a guarantee of sales, or an exhaustive manual for every advertising platform. It favors LinkedIn as a first social example because it fits the developer-professional context, and compares other channels in the workbook.

Current, platform-specific statements and the introductory U.S. compliance discussion link to primary documentation. Strategy frameworks, fictional case studies, and planning schedules are instructional synthesis. Sources do not establish a guaranteed conversion rate or a universal posting formula. There is no fabricated creator audit or claim to have tested campaigns in your accounts.

Tax, intellectual-property, privacy, and consumer-law obligations depend on the actual business and jurisdictions. The course identifies implementation questions rather than deciding those obligations for an unseen business. Recheck current official requirements before configuring campaigns or selling internationally.

## Suggested first step

Read Lesson 1, then fill in the business hypothesis and buyer-situation fields in the first workbook. You do not need to choose software or buy ads first. Your initial task is to identify one problem you can teach well and collect evidence that people want the proposed help.
